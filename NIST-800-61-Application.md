# NIST SP 800-61 Incident Response Mapping

This section maps the GREY VEIL investigation to the NIST SP 800-61 Computer Security Incident Handling Guide. The investigation aligned closely with standard incident response phases and demonstrates how telemetry, threat hunting, and response actions supported the investigation lifecycle.

| NIST Phase | Application to Investigation |
|---|---|
| **Preparation** | Microsoft Sentinel, Sysmon, FortiGate VPN telemetry, and Microsoft Defender for Endpoint were configured and available before the incident. These tools provided centralized visibility and enabled investigators to collect process, file, registry, endpoint, and network telemetry across the environment. |
| **Detection and Analysis** | KQL-based threat hunting identified suspicious VPN activity involving `s.brandt`, authentication from anonymized infrastructure, credential access attempts, WMIC lateral movement, PortProxy persistence, and exfiltration behavior. Correlation of telemetry across multiple data sources enabled investigators to reconstruct the attack timeline and identify compromised systems and accounts. |
| **Containment** | Immediate containment actions focused on stopping attacker activity and preventing additional compromise. Actions included isolating `WS-ENG04`, `SRV-DC01`, and `SRV-FILES02`; disabling compromised accounts `s.brandt` and `m.richter`; removing unauthorized `netsh interface portproxy` configurations; blocking outbound communication to `cdn-telemetry.cloud-endpoint.net`; terminating suspicious WMIC and PowerShell activity; resetting credentials; invalidating VPN sessions; and identifying persistence mechanisms across affected systems. **Objective:** Prevent further attacker movement and stop ongoing access.
| **Eradication and Recovery** | Eradication and recovery activities focused on removing attacker artifacts and restoring trusted operations. Actions included removing the malicious `C:\Windows\Temp\McAfee_Logs` staging directory, deleting PortProxy registry entries, performing malware and persistence scans, rotating privileged and service account credentials, rebuilding compromised systems when required, validating system integrity before reconnecting systems to production, and reviewing VPN configurations to strengthen authentication controls. **Objective:** Remove attacker presence and safely restore business operations.|
| **Post-Incident Activity / Lessons Learned** | The investigation identified detection gaps involving WMIC activity, credential access monitoring, PortProxy persistence, and exfiltration visibility. Although attackers attempted anti-forensics actions by clearing Windows Security logs, Sysmon telemetry remained intact and enabled reconstruction of the attack lifecycle. Lessons learned emphasized centralized logging, behavioral analytics, stronger identity protection, and improved automated response capabilities. |

## Key Observation

The GREY VEIL investigation demonstrated that layered telemetry and centralized visibility are critical during incident response. While attackers attempted to remove evidence, alternate telemetry sources preserved investigative visibility and supported complete reconstruction of attacker activity.
